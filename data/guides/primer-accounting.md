# Accounting Primer (original summary for IB Copilot)

This is an original, plain-English primer written for the IB Copilot demo so the app has
grounded content without redistributing any purchased prep material. Replace or supplement
it with your own licensed guides locally.

## The three financial statements
- **Income Statement** shows profitability over a period: revenue minus expenses down to
  net income. It is accrual-based, so it records revenue when earned and expenses when
  incurred, not when cash moves.
- **Balance Sheet** is a snapshot at a point in time: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders'
  Equity. It must always balance.
- **Cash Flow Statement** reconciles net income to the actual change in cash, in three
  sections: cash from operations, investing, and financing.

## How the statements link
Net income from the Income Statement flows to the top of the Cash Flow Statement and into
Retained Earnings on the Balance Sheet. Non-cash items (depreciation, amortization,
stock-based compensation) are added back in operating cash flow. Changes in working capital
(receivables, inventory, payables) adjust operating cash flow. The ending cash balance from
the Cash Flow Statement becomes the cash line on the Balance Sheet.

## Walk through: a $10 increase in depreciation (35% tax)
- Income Statement: pre-tax income falls $10; at 35% tax, net income falls $6.50.
- Cash Flow: net income down $6.50, add back $10 of depreciation, so cash rises $3.50.
- Balance Sheet: cash up $3.50, PP&E down $10 (net), so assets fall $6.50; retained earnings
  down $6.50 on the other side. It balances.

## Working capital and free cash flow
Working capital = current assets (ex-cash) minus current liabilities (ex-debt). An increase
in working capital uses cash. Unlevered free cash flow ≈ EBIT × (1 − tax) + D&A − capex −
increase in working capital.

## Common pitfalls
Confusing accrual revenue with cash; forgetting the tax effect on non-cash items; and
forgetting that the Balance Sheet must balance after any change.
