# Valuation Primer (original summary for IB Copilot)

Original, plain-English primer for the demo so the app has grounded valuation content
without redistributing purchased material.

## Equity value vs. enterprise value
- **Equity value** (market cap) = share price × diluted shares. It is the value to equity
  holders only.
- **Enterprise value (EV)** = equity value + total debt + preferred + minority interest −
  cash. EV is the value of the core business to all capital providers and is
  capital-structure neutral, which is why it is used in many multiples.
- Why subtract cash: an acquirer could use the target's cash to pay down the purchase price.

## Valuation multiples
- **EV / EBITDA** and **EV / EBIT** pair an EV-based numerator with a metric available to all
  investors (before interest). **P / E** pairs equity value with net income (after interest),
  so it is an equity multiple.
- Use EV multiples when comparing companies with different capital structures; use P/E for
  equity-holder perspective.

## The three main methods
1. **Comparable companies** — value based on trading multiples of similar public companies.
   Quick and market-based, but no two companies are identical.
2. **Precedent transactions** — multiples paid in past M&A deals; includes a control premium,
   so values tend to be higher.
3. **Discounted cash flow (DCF)** — intrinsic value from projected cash flows.

## DCF mechanics
- Project unlevered free cash flow for 5–10 years.
- Discount at the **WACC** (weighted average cost of debt and equity; cost of equity via CAPM
  = risk-free + beta × equity risk premium).
- Add a **terminal value** (Gordon growth: FCF × (1+g) / (WACC − g); or an exit EBITDA
  multiple), discounted back.
- Sum the present values to get enterprise value; subtract net debt for equity value.
- DCF is sensitive to WACC, terminal growth, and margin assumptions — always sanity-check
  the terminal value's share of total value.
