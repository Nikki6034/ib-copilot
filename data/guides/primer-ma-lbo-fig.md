# M&A, LBO & FIG Primer (original summary for IB Copilot)

Original, plain-English primer for the demo so the app has grounded M&A, LBO and FIG content
without redistributing purchased material.

## M&A and merger models
- **Accretion / dilution**: a deal is accretive if the acquirer's pro-forma EPS rises and
  dilutive if it falls. Quick rule for an all-stock deal: it is accretive if the acquirer's
  P/E is higher than the target's (effectively acquiring cheaper earnings).
- **Consideration mix**: cash (often funded with debt) is usually cheaper than stock when
  interest rates are below the earnings yield, but uses balance-sheet capacity.
- **Synergies**: cost synergies (removing duplicate overhead) are more credible than revenue
  synergies. Buyers justify control premiums with expected synergies.

## LBO fundamentals
- A **leveraged buyout** uses significant debt to acquire a company; the target's cash flows
  repay the debt.
- Returns are driven by (1) debt paydown, (2) EBITDA growth, and (3) multiple expansion.
- Key tests: can the company's free cash flow service the debt? Typical return metric is IRR
  (often targeting ~20%+) and MOIC (multiple of invested capital).
- Ideal LBO targets have stable cash flows, low existing debt, and a clear exit.

## FIG (Financial Institutions Group)
Banks and insurers are balance-sheet-driven and regulated, so standard EV/EBITDA and DCF do
not apply cleanly.
- **Banks** are valued on **P / Book Value**, **P / Tangible Book Value (P/TBV)**, and
  **P / E**, judged against **ROE / ROTCE**. Key drivers: net interest margin (NIM), loan
  growth, credit quality (provisions, charge-offs), efficiency ratio, and regulatory capital
  (e.g., CET1). A **dividend discount model** is often used instead of a standard DCF because
  "free cash flow" is hard to define for a bank.
- **Insurers** are valued on book value and metrics like the combined ratio (for P&C).
- **Asset / wealth managers** are valued on AUM, fee rates, and fee-related earnings (FRE).
- In bank M&A, the premium is often framed against **tangible book value** and judged by the
  **TBV earnback period** and **ROTCE accretion**.
